# **Introduction**


Chicago is the third-largest city in the USA and has long been regarded by many as one of the most dangerous cities in the country. This reputation, shaped by both history and Hollywood, often centers around gang violence and organized crime.

But is this image still accurate today? What does recent data reveal about the actual crime situation in Chicago? Where is crime still a problem, and who is most affected by it? When have improvements or shifts occurred, and why do these patterns persist or change?

This data story investigates recent crime numbers in Chicago and explores whether everyday life in the city has improved compared to other major US cities. It aims to go beyond the stereotypes by examining how crime is distributed across neighborhoods, and what role structural factors and policing strategies play in shaping the city’s safety landscape.

## Perspective 1: General View on Crime in Chicago

While Chicago is often known for high crime rates, there have been positive developments in recent years.

### Argument 1 – Gradual Decrease in Crime:
 
 Over the past few years, the total number of reported crimes in Chicago has shown a slow but steady decline. This trend became especially noticeable during the COVID-19 pandemic[5], when lockdowns and restrictions led to fewer people on the streets and less opportunity for criminal activity. Although the decline is not drastic, it indicates a positive direction for overall public safety in the city.

### Argument 2 – Stable Trends in Common Offenses:
 
 When analyzing the types of crimes committed, it becomes clear that common offenses like theft, battery, and property damage have remained relatively consistent in percentage terms. This suggests that, while crime is still present, large-scale spikes in specific categories are being prevented[1]. Such stability could be a result of ongoing prevention efforts, law enforcement strategies, and community programs.


## Perspective 2: Crime Distribution per Police District in Chicago

Crime is not evenly distributed across Chicago, with certain police districts facing significantly higher incident rates.

### Argument 1 – High-Risk Areas Identified:
 
 Certain police districts, such as District 8, 7, and 6, report significantly more crimes than other parts of the city. These areas are often characterized by higher population density, socioeconomic challenges, and complex social issues, like lower income, that contribute to elevated crime levels[4]. Identifying these high-risk zones helps authorities allocate resources more effectively to the areas where they are most needed.
